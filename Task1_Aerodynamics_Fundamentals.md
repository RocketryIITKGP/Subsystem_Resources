Task 1: Getting Thorough with Aerodynamics Fundamentals For Propulsion  
\
This task is important from the aspect of understanding the flow of combustion exhaust from the combustor. Just reacting the fuel and oxidiser together is not sufficient. The exhaust must also convert the thermal energy into useful kinetic energy, which is required for thrust generation.  
\
Hence, for rocket propulsion, this aerodynamic understanding is also necessary. This is not the same as the aerodynamics of cars or flow through pipes. This is compressible flow. The flow of water is incompressible, meaning the density of water does not change. The flow of air is not like that. Air is a compressible fluid, and that changes how air exhaust behaves. The thermodynamics involved is not about piston work; the thermodynamics involved here is for a flow process.  
\
To understand everything, here are some really well-written fundamental books on aerodynamics and gas dynamics (the study of high-speed or high-temperature air flow). These books have information about concepts we will be concerned about.  
\
It is NOT important here to memorise the equations or any theories.  
\
From "Fundamentals of Aerodynamics", it is necessary to UNDERSTAND the basics of fluid mechanics, how fluids behave, how the flow around bodies or inside ducts is different, etc.  
\
From "Elements of Gas Dynamics", it is necessary to UNDERSTAND the physical significance of those equations, what a shock is, what the Mach number is, how shock formation affects the flow, and all such things, which you will discover while reading.  
\
From "Modern Compressible Flows", read and UNDERSTAND the "One-Dimensional Flow" Chapter, talking about flow acceleration and deceleration through ducts, required for designing a nozzle with Physics, and not 'Vibe'.  
\
The content is huge, obviously. Comprehending everything is not required; rather, it is necessary to comprehend the essential portions sufficiently to apply the concepts to the design part, thereby reducing experimental effort, cost, and time.
